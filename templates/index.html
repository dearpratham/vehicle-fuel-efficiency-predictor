📖 Understanding the Factors

⛽ Fuel Type

Petrol engines are common and affordable but consume more fuel. Diesel engines provide ~20-30% better fuel efficiency due to higher compression ratios and energy-dense fuel. CNG is the cheapest per km but requires larger tanks and may reduce boot space.

🔧 Engine Displacement (CC)

Measured in Cubic Centimeters (CC), this indicates the total volume of all cylinders. Smaller engines (800-1200 CC) are more fuel-efficient. Larger engines (2000+ CC) produce more power but consume more fuel. A 1.2L engine in a hatchback vs a 3.0L in an SUV can differ by 10+ km/l.

🔩 Number of Cylinders

Cylinders are chambers where fuel combustion occurs. 3-cylinder engines are fuel-efficient (small cars). 4-cylinder engines balance power and efficiency (most common). 6-8 cylinders provide high power but reduced efficiency (SUVs, sports cars).

⚙️ Number of Gears

More gears allow the engine to stay in its optimal RPM range across speeds. 5-speed is standard for budget cars. 6-7 speed improves highway efficiency by ~3-5%. 8+ speed transmissions offer the finest gear ratio optimization.

🐎 Power (HP)

Horsepower measures the engine's output. 60-100 HP is typical for city hatchbacks. 100-170 HP suits sedans and compact SUVs. 200+ HP is for performance-oriented vehicles. Higher power generally means higher fuel consumption, as larger/tuned engines burn more fuel.

🕹️ Transmission Type

Manual — Driver-controlled, efficient when driven well.
AMT — Automated Manual; affordable, similar efficiency to manual.
DCT — Dual Clutch; fast shifts, very fuel-efficient.
iVT — Intelligent Variable; seamless, best for city driving.
Torque Converter — Smooth but slightly less efficient.

🚗 Vehicle Body Type

Hatchback — Lightest, best efficiency, compact city car.
Sedan — Moderate weight, good aerodynamics, balanced.
Sub-compact SUV — Raised hatchback, slightly less efficient.
SUV — Heavy, high drag, significantly lower efficiency.
Off-Road — Heaviest, worst aerodynamics, lowest efficiency.

🛣️ Driving Conditions

City — Frequent stops, idling, and low-speed driving use 15-20% more fuel than mixed conditions. Highway — Steady cruising at optimal RPM is 15% more efficient. Mixed — A blend of both; this is the standard test condition used by manufacturers.
